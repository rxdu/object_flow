# Audit area 5: durability, recovery, upgrade, events and release (promises D and E)

Checkout `698034b`, release binary `objectflow-service 0.2.0, … commit 698034b`, 2026-10-14. Rubric: ADR-0204 §1 to §5 (`docs/adr/0204-…md`: D at lines 82-87, E at 101, severities 111-136, evidence 156-159, decision 9.4 at 218). Paths below are relative to the checkout unless they start `a5/`, which is `scratchpad/audit/a5/`. Every experiment is a script in `a5/work/`, run as `cd a5/work && python3 <script> <args>` against the release binary on copies of `g2.sqlite` or stores I made with `--create`; each started process was recorded in `a5/work/pids/` and stopped by its pid, and `ps` shows none left. I ran no cargo, no build, no gate and no suite; a test named as "holding" an item was read, not run.

## 1. How the area was enumerated, and why it is complete

**D, durability.** I enumerated every place the engine writes the store, from three sources that must agree:
- every SQL write site in the core (`grep` of `begin_write!`, `commit()`, `unchecked_transaction`, `execute_batch`, `UPDATE`/`DELETE` in `store.rs`, `release.rs`, `import.rs`, `request.rs`): request `transact` (store.rs:2463-2519, requests, imports, erasures, submits), publish `install` (store.rs:2689-3157), `maintain` (2301-2364), `record_attempt` (2369-2376), drawn sequence numbers after a refusal (2498-2505), `acknowledge` (2434-2461), store creation (2033-2048, 2264-2288), index DDL at every open (2072-2085), the upgrade's steps (release.rs:189-298), and the three checkpoints (`after_erasure` 2154-2183, `truncate_log` 2189-2210, the stop's `checkpoint` 2219-2224);
- the service's write routes (`WRITE_ROUTES`, main.rs:475-482) and its start and stop sequence (`serve`, main.rs:5163-5476; `store_file` 4966-5106; `hold_store` 5109-5133; `stopping` 4228-4258; `cut_back` 4337-4405);
- the operator's acts the guides prescribe or forbid on the files (`operate.md` 278-353: backup, check, restore, upgrade; 359-361 stopping; 7-11 the files).

Each write site was crossed with each way it can be interrupted (SIGKILL, SIGTERM within and past the 30 s grace, a full volume, power loss by syscall trace) and each way the files can change under the service (moved, removed, overwritten in place, `-wal` removed, a backup copied over). That set is complete for D because every byte the store holds is written through one of the listed sites, and every interruption the trust model keeps inside (§2: everything but undetected operator acts) is in the cross product.

**E, events.** Delivery is `pull` (subscription.rs:41-102), `export` and its seven sources (export.rs:44-170: `log`, `<T>.transitions`, `.intervals`, `.attempts`, `.attempt_counts`, `.labels`, an observation kind), `acknowledge` (store.rs:2434-2461), and the built-in `Subscription` type's filter (subscription.rs:116-191). I took every cursor each accepts (wire.md:148-150, 168-177; errors.md:130) and every commit that writes a row a source reads. The latter is where the class of V7 was found: every `UPDATE` or `DELETE` of `of_interval` (store.rs:2736, 2763, 4759) moves or removes a row that the `.intervals` export pages by its last writing event.

**Release.** `.github/workflows/{check,release,advisories}.yml`, `scripts/check-all`, `core/scripts/check`, `.cargo/config.toml` with `core/crates/objectflow-core/build.rs`, and `release.rs` (`RELEASES`, the fingerprint test). For "what a 0.2.0-to-1.0.0 upgrade needs" I read every input the upgrade or the open re-reads: the release table, the recorded sources, the built-ins flag, the fixtures.

**Left out, and why.** The real 0.1.0 fixture `.sqlite` files (the rule forbids opening a `.sqlite` I did not create; I made a 0.1.0-shaped store instead, see U1, and read the test that holds the real ones). The six-year store (no D or E question here depends on size). Power loss itself (cannot be cut on a shared machine; the per-commit flush was traced instead, W2). PostgreSQL and the racer (no SQLite race exists: writers are serial). The sandbox's durability (an in-memory store with no promise; §4 grades the sandbox alone minor at most). P's own reach (what an erasure must remove) is area 1's; I graded only where a crash or a restore is the cause.

## 3. The broken items, most severe first

No blocker: no acknowledged write was lost in normal operation, and no event went undelivered in normal operation. Eleven major rows in six classes (V6 repeats B5), then the minors.

1. **B4 + B5 (V6): a restore hands out ids, positions, transactions, flow sequence numbers and keys again, and honours pre-restore cursors once the log passes them** (D5, D4, E1, E2). Smallest fix: ADR-0204 decision 9.4 as decided, widened to every `of_sequence` row: the restore's procedure gains one act (a start flag, say `--restored`, or a `maintain` task run before serving) that advances `of_counter`'s object, position and txn and every sequence's `next_value` past a stated margin, recorded as the operator's event; `pull`, `export` and `acknowledge` refuse `invalid_cursor` any cursor inside the skipped gap, not only one past the end; and `operate.md`'s restore gains the step. Refuses what 0.2.0 accepted: a cursor in the gap, which 0.2.0 honoured once the log passed it.
2. **S3, S4, S5 (D688's class): writes acknowledged after the service has found the store unreadable, its `-wal` gone, or a read malformed** (D1, D3). Smallest fix: one latch in the service, set by `/health`'s `store_unreadable`, by any `SQLITE_CORRUPT` or `SQLITE_NOTADB` from any connection, and by a change of the `-wal`'s device and inode (compared at each write as the store's are, main.rs:551-570); while it is set, every write and read answers `503 store_unreadable` and the stop skips its checkpoint. Refuses: writes after the detection, which 0.2.0 answered `200` and then lost.
3. **V7a, V7b, V7c: the `.intervals` export never retracts a row a later commit deleted or re-keyed** (E1). Smallest fix: export each row with `entered_position` (V8), and write a tombstone, `(object_id, dimension, entered_position, position)`, for every interval row the correction merge deletes (store.rs:4759), the publish sets aside (2763) or renames (2736), exported in cursor order as `{"retracted": true, …}`. Refuses nothing; adds a field and rows within `/v1`'s rule; its table needs a migration in 1.0.0's release entry (U8).
4. **V9 (D689): a subscription whose filter selects nothing is taken** (E1). Smallest fix: refuse `subscribe` `invalid_input` where `target_type` names no type of the installed version (exact case), `transitions` names one no selected type declares, a duration is not positive, or `dead_after` is below `lagging_after`; written in the engine's input check, not as a declared condition of the built-in type, so the built-ins' fingerprint stays and 0.2.0 stores still upgrade (U5). Refuses: those subscriptions, which 0.2.0 took.
5. **C3: a start refused after the store is opened changes the file** (D3). Smallest fix: bind the main listener and `--metrics-bind` before `store_file`, `hold_store` and `Store::open` (main.rs:5205-5300, 5406). Refuses nothing.
6. **W6b (P, area 1's): an erasure's truncation due at a kill is forgotten** (P1). Smallest fix: `Store::open` runs `wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)` before serving where the start found the `-wal` holding frames, after the quick check passed. Refuses nothing.

Minors, each with its smallest fix (none refuses what 0.2.0 accepted unless said):
- **C4** a store due an upgrade starts unchecked: run `Store::checked_file` in both arms (main.rs:5219-5244). Refuses: a damaged store at an earlier release, which 0.2.0 started and upgraded.
- **W11** a crash during `--create`: build the store in `<store>.creating` in one transaction, then rename it into place and sync the directory; remove a stale `.creating` at the next `--create`.
- **C5** the restore's advice on every refused start: give it only where the `-wal` holds frames, as the start's note does (main.rs:4974-4990).
- **W8b** `acknowledge` outside `begin_write!`: write it through `begin_write!`, refusing `stale`/`retries_exhausted` as a request does.
- **V5** `.attempt_counts`: refuse `invalid_cursor` a day after the store's today. Refuses: such a cursor, which 0.2.0 answered empty.
- **V8** interval rows without an entry position: add `entered_position` (part of fix 3).
- **C7** a stop with its socket gone: say it and go on to the checkpoint and "stopped".
- **B2** the guide's backup and check: open the source `mode=ro` in the one-off command too, and have the check read the release table.
- **B6** and **V12** the guides' wrong sentences on `pull`'s cursor: correct both, and have `check-guides.py` hold them.
- **U6** and **U7** the release's identity: widen the fingerprint to a fixture library's tables, and record the build's commit with the release.
