# ADR-0195: The eighth review's findings, decided

- **Status:** **Decided** 2026-10-13 on the author's standing instruction, "continue the loop to get the stack ready for production, follow your recommendations", each decision the recommendation stated here; built the same day
- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Refined by:** ADR-0196 — a change's parse and checks linear in what it holds, a stall looked for on a tick of its own, another's change moved on only by an approver, the operator in any case.
- **Refines:** ADR-0194 decision 4 (the log's cut), ADR-0192 decision 6 (an identity, one actor), ADR-0187 decision 1 (who approves a version), ADR-0175 (the evaluator's transport), ADR-0190 decision 2 (the read pools)
- **Relates to:** `design/production-review.md` §12; `guides/operate.md`; `guides/agents.md`; `guides/wire.md`

## Context

The eighth production review (`production-review.md` §12) found no blocker and two majors. ERASE-WAL-DRYRUN: a change's dry run ran its checks inside its read of the store, and its simulation's world did not carry the stop an urgent cut sends, so an erasure's values stayed in the store's files for as long as a dry run ran, 38.7 s for a check of a large change, where the guide promises about five seconds. CASE-PAIR: decision 6 of ADR-0192 made an identity name one actor without the case of its letters, and nothing in a store made by 0.2.0, which took `EMP0000@…` beside `emp0000@…`, could then be decided: the two actors could not leave nor be erased, `identity_taken` refusing each, and every publish was refused `decision_unresolved`, its report asking for an admission both checkers refused, since neither counted a generated invariant among a type's.

## Decision

1. **A dry run's checks hold no read of the store, and its impact is stopped as every read is.** The service runs a change's dry run, its submit's or refresh's, its preview and a check of its submit, as three steps under its one slot and its deadline: the change read; its checks and examples run with no read of the store open, since they read only the installed version and the change's text; and its impact over the objects on a second read, its simulation's world carrying the read's stop and deadline, so an urgent cut stops it, answered `503 read_timeout` to be sent again. Where the installed version moved between the two reads, the checks run again on the second. A `check` of a change's submit takes the dry run so prepared, as the submit does. (ERASE-WAL-DRYRUN)
2. **A migration may admit `identity_taken`,** both checkers counting it among the invariants of a type whose identity declares an actor kind, so a store carried from a release that took two identities equal but for case is decided by a publish whose migration admits it; the two actors then leave and are erased as any admitted violation lets them, and a later version may give one of them another identity, discharging it. A store holding such a pair says so at its start, by the objects' ids and never the identities, with the remedy, and the guide's upgrade says it. A request naming the identity in a third case resolves to the actor made first; one naming it exactly, to the actor holding it. An import still admits no `identity_taken` (ADR-0192 decision 6): it brings data in, where a migration decides what a store already holds. (CASE-PAIR)
3. **The evaluator's transport follows no redirect**: a `3xx` is an answer that is not `200`, so the evaluator is unknown and its guard refused `temporal`, where a `302` sent the call to a host the operator never configured and its answer decided a guard. (EV-REDIRECT)
4. **Where no module declares `publishing.taken_by`, an agent's approval of the next version is refused**, in both backends, `invariant_violated`, `publish_taken_by`, `delegable`, as a declared rule refuses it, and any other kind's is taken: where none declared who approves, the agent the version declares approved its own change. An actor of the `agent` kind exists only where the installed version declares one, so the rule reads the approver's kind. A declared rule is unchanged, and the operator always may approve. The checkers' notice `publishing_open` stays, saying the default decides who approves.
5. **An object's history and events wait in a pool of their own**, `objects`, `--max-reads` at once, never behind the worklists' whole-type scans, where a person's history waited 2.4 to 4.8 s; a page of either is bounded by 8 MiB, so the pool bounds their memory. An agent's reads stay in the agents' pool. The guide's memory rule counts every pool.
6. **A stalled write is said as it stalls, and failing writes are said.** The store tells the service since when its writes have waited for another process's lock, set at a write's first retry, kept through the writes refused after their retries, and cleared once a write takes the lock. `writer_held_seconds` counts from that first retry while it stands; `/health` says `writes_stalled` while a write is held in that wait, as before after a refusal, within the minute; and the log says the wait once, within 5 s of its start. `/health` says `writes_failing` once three or more writes in a row were answered `500` and none was answered otherwise since, the last within the minute, where every write failed `500` behind a `200`.
7. **The minors are fixed**: a store refused at its start beside a `-wal` or `-shm` says to restore the copy alone on every path, the check that the file holds a store and its opening among them, where a backup restored beside a later `-wal` was refused with SQLite's words alone; a request's `actor`, on every route that takes one, is an object of an `id` and a `principal` or a `400`, in both backends; `wire.md` names the request that records a label, and `agents.md` lists recording labels among what a gateway decides for an agent; the guides say to make the store as the service's user and stop that first process, give the unit `Restart=on-failure`, warn that a restore hands out again the ids and positions handed out since the backup and how to restart a subscriber, say a source build names its commit only with `OBJECTFLOW_COMMIT`, give a stalled write's wait as about 40 s and a client's write timeout above 45 s, the `-wal`'s peak as about 300 MiB, and an alert on the rate of `5xx`; DESIGN.md §2's sizing sentence is brought to PRD N3 and the measured stores; CI installs `cargo-audit` at a pinned version, 0.22.2.
8. **Unchanged**: an integer written as a string with a sign or leading zeros, `"+5"` or `"007"`, is the integer it writes; an unread answer is bounded in time by `--idle-timeout`, and in memory by the connection cap and the pages' 8 MiB; a filter on a value derived from a set reads its type on each page, as the guide says, the remedy a stored and indexed value; and an `export` page may pass its limit by the rows that share its last cursor (ADR-0179), which `wire.md` now says.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A dry run on one read with the read's mark released during its checks** (decision 1): SQLite holds a read transaction's snapshot until it ends, so releasing it is ending it; two reads say so plainly.
- **Stopping a dry run's checks at an urgent cut** (decision 1): the checks hold no store read once apart, so there is nothing to stop; their cost is the dry-run slot's, under its deadline.
- **An engine release whose upgrade admits legacy pairs itself** (decision 2): a release is the author's act, and an admission made for the operator decides what the operator should; **refusing to start** such a store, which strands it; **a maintenance task renaming an identity**, an operation of the wire for a case a migration already covers.
- **Leaving publishing open to agents and the checker's notice to warn** (decision 4), which the review measured failing open; **closing it to every kind but `human`**, which would refuse a service a deployment approves versions by; **dropping the notice** once the default is safe, where a version that says nothing of who approves is still worth a line.
- **Raising `--max-reads` in the guide** (decision 5), which the review measured past the guide's own memory bound.
- **Inferring a stall from how long the writer is held** (decision 6): a publish holds it 9 to 15 s on six years with no other process in the way; only the store sees a retry.
- **An `export` page cut before the cursor whose rows would pass its limit** (decision 8), first written as decision 7's fix: a page would then end short while rows remain, which `wire.md` tells a consumer means it has caught up, so it would wait out its poll for nothing; passing the limit by one cursor's rows breaks neither the order nor that signal.

## Consequences

- `/v1` refuses what it took: an agent's approval of a version under a version that declares no `publishing`, a request's `actor` of another JSON type or with another key. Before 1.0.0 this costs no promise (ADR-0180).
- A store carried from 0.2.0 with a case pair needs one publish, its migration admitting `identity_taken`; the 0.2.0 upgrade fixture, the author's when the release is cut, holds such a pair.
- `/health` has one more code, `writes_failing`, and says `writes_stalled` from a write's first retry; the reads have a fifth pool, `objects`, in `/metrics`.
- Each fix is held by a test that fails without it, and the dry run's files were measured again beside an erasure: a check of 4,500 types, 18.7 s, kept nothing erased in either file a second after the erasure, where the eighth review's build kept it in the `-wal` for the whole of its 20.3 s check (`defects.md` D618). The review's path to `writes_failing`, a backup copied over the live file in place, was not reproduced: of three copies tried, two failed `/health` too and one served the copy's state, so the test fails writes by a trigger another process adds.
