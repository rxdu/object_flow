# ADR-0197: The tenth review's findings, decided

- **Status:** **Decided** 2026-10-13 on the author's standing instruction, "continue the loop to get the stack ready for production, follow your recommendations", each decision the recommendation stated here; built the same day
- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Refined by:** ADR-0198 — an expression's unfolding of derived attributes bounded, the dry run's checks held to its deadline, a derived value computed once in an evaluation, and an export page read by the store's indexes. ADR-0201 — a derived value past its bound of hops not run, never none.
- **Refines:** ADR-0196 decisions 1 and 2; ADR-0179 (paging); the derived attributes of `flow-format.md` §4 and the proposal's approval of `DESIGN.md` §9
- **Relates to:** `design/production-review.md` §14; `guides/operate.md`; `guides/wire.md`; `guides/agents.md`; `guides/integrate.md`

## Context

The tenth production review (`production-review.md` §14) found, through its adversary, a blocker: a derived attribute reading itself through a reference, `depth: parent.depth + 1` on a `Node` whose `parent` is a `Node`, which both checkers accepted, overflowed the stack and aborted the whole service on a plain `get` once a chain ran to about 4,000 nodes or two nodes made a cycle, and every restart died at the next read of either. It found four majors beside it: a subscription's lag read 256 events whole on every `get`, and `pull` and `export` read their limit before cutting a page at its bytes, so a page's answer was bounded and its memory not (four exports at once 4.4 GB); a proposal executed on its object as approval found it, so an agent proposed sending USD 5.00, grew the payment, and a person's approval sent USD 5,000,000; a panic in a request kept its connection's place among the open ones for good, twelve answering every request `503 busy`, the panic coming from an identifier's width the checkers accepted; and a change's checks were still quadratic in a type's transitions, 8,000 holding every write 71 s through the approval. The operator found one major: a log reader that stopped reading froze every request, `/health` included, two requests each holding standard error's lock while the runtime has two threads. Measuring the fixes found two more of the checker's kind: a chain of derived attributes typed in cubic time, 1,000 taking 171 s, and a dry run on a populated store growing about 13 ms a type, each object's lookup by key walking every type of the library.

## Decision

1. **A derived attribute reading itself through a reference is a cycle, refused by both checkers, and a value its evaluation reaches past 64 references has none.** The checkers' search for derived attributes reading each other counts a type's own derived attribute read through a reference, finds the reads by each expression's names after a dot, and visits each attribute once, iteratively, so neither a long chain nor many paths makes it long or deep; their typing goes through at most 64 derived attributes, past which a type is unknown. At run time an evaluation inside 64 derived attributes, each reached through a reference, gives the next no value, so a store a version installed before the checkers refused it answers its reads. (the blocker)
2. **A subscription's selection reads its events' heads, and whole events only for the page.** Its lag reads the first selected event's recorded time and never the event; a page of `pull` reads its events one at a time to its 8 MiB; a page of the log's or a type's transitions' `export` reads the log 16 events at a time to its bytes; a page that reaches its bytes says `more`. (major 1, the rest of the ninth review's N-9)
3. **An approval may name `target_version`, the version of the proposed object its approver read, and is refused `stale` where the object has moved since**, in both backends; one naming none runs on the object as approval finds it, as the design says. `target_version` on any other request is a `400`. The guides tell an approval screen to send the version it showed. (major 2)
4. **A request's panic is answered `500`, its connection's place let go however its task ends, and an identifier's width is at most 20 digits**, refused by both checkers past it and refused as not run at run time where an earlier version declared one, never a panic. (major 3)
5. **A change's checks are linear in what it declares, and a dry run's impact in the types it adds.** The checkers read a type in place where they cloned it whole for each transition, attribute and finding, and describe each expression's readable names where they copied them for each; a world finds each type's family once where each lookup by key walked every type. On release builds 8,000 transitions on one type check in 0.57 s, where 70 s; a chain of 3,000 derived attributes in 0.81 s, where 1,000 took 171 s; a change adding 1,000 types to the 37k-object store dry-runs in 2.2 s, where 14.5 s. A test holds each growth. (major 4 and the measurement's)
6. **The log is written by a thread of its own**, fed a bounded queue of 4,096 lines, a line past it dropped and counted, so no request waits on the log however its reader stalls; the thread marks the line it writes, which `/health` reads as `log_blocked` past 5 s, and the service drains the queue for up to 2 s as it ends. (the operator's major, LOG-FREEZE)
7. **The minors**: an agent's `submit` of another's draft is refused as its other moves on another's change are (ADR-0196 decision 5), in both backends; a failed `--create` in a directory the service cannot write says what to do; a late dry run says it may have waited behind another's; the guides gain a call over the Unix socket, a backup schedule with an off-host copy, its verification and its expiry, a `maintain` schedule, the log's volume, an export page's memory, the approval's hold under load, a label's `forget` and `corrects` and the drafting of changes on the gateway's list, and that an idempotency key holds no personal data.
8. **Unchanged**: a backup copied over a running service's file in place is not detected (ADR-0196 decision 10), the guide's restore stopping the service first; a derived money or metric past 18 digits is answered, being computed and never stored; `history` with `follow` reads the whole supersession group on each page; an imported identifier is taken as the other system gave it; a claim of more lines than its cascade's limit is neither erasable nor withdrawable, `over_limit` saying so; write fairness between actors is the gateway's; a restore hands out ids again, as the guide says; `/metrics`' names and the log's lines are not promised.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Pinning a proposal to its object's version at filing** (decision 3), built first: a proposal may be filed before its object reaches the state its transition leaves, a job's handover proposed while the job is still `OPEN` and approved once it has started, which the design and a test hold; a pin refused that. The approver's own reading is what an approval must hold to.
- **Refusing every derived attribute that reads through a reference of its own type** (decision 1): one reading a parent's stored value, `parent.code`, reaches no cycle; only a derived attribute reaching itself is.
- **A deadline inside the checks** (decision 5): it bounds a check found slow and leaves it slow; the checks are made linear, and tests hold their growth.
- **A larger worker pool for the log** (decision 6): any number of threads blocked on one pipe stop as two did; only a writer of its own takes the wait off the requests.

## Consequences

- `/v1/request` and `/v1/check` take `target_version`, promised in `wire-v1.json`. Before 1.0.0 this costs no promise (ADR-0180).
- A flow whose derived attribute reads itself through a reference, or whose identifier is wider than 20 digits, is refused; one installed before keeps serving, its recursion valueless past 64.
- Each fix is held by a test that fails without it (`defects.md` D625 to D630).
