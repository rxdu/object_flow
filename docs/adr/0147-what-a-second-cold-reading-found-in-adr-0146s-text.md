## Context

ADR-0146 decided what a cold reading found unsettled in what the engine does beyond a request, and its decisions were written into the specification, the checkers and the reference runner between 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-04. Four readers with no context then read that text, each asked whether two careful builders following it could build engines that differ: recording, correcting and dating a request and evaluators; erasure, proposals, delivery and the model's details; publishing with a migration and the import; and metrics. They found 133 findings, 124 distinct, recorded as they wrote them in `cold-reading-2-review.md` §1 to §4. Most needed one sentence; some were contradictions between documents, three were things that could not be built as written (no shape to file a proposal or manage a subscription, no way to express a refused `pull`, one transition of two kinds), and three changed what the engine promises, which the author decided.

## Decision

1. **`time_in` is real elapsed time everywhere.** It totals every span in the state, a current one up to `now`, in a guard, a derivation and a metric row alike. Nothing accruing on a finished object stays a rule of a span's `.duration` in a metric, and covers only a span still current.
2. **A proposal keeps the request's occurred time and context, and its bound is measured at filing.** The check against the current interval of what it changes runs at approval. The idempotency key stays with each request.
3. **An engine upgrade that changes the built-ins is its own approved change.** `upgrade` has a dry run whose impact report says what the new built-ins change and which installed flows fail; running it is the operator's recorded act; it installs a version naming the release as its cause, and supersedes every open `DeclarationChange` as a publish does. ADR-0105's rule that built-ins change only through a publish becomes: only through a reported, recorded act.
4. **Recording and dating** (review §6, 1 to 15): a correction of another subject is `invalid input`, and names its subject as every recording does; `subject_open` reads the subject as the request found it, a stated exception to step 6; a correction's `.occurred_at` is the corrected observation's and its event is the request's; every transition the request takes, a creation's included, records the request's occurred time, and only the requested one checks a bound; a caused transition's interval failure refuses as a caused refusal does; "changes" means ends different; `now` is the recorded time; the occurred time belongs to what an idempotency key identifies; an attempt is dated by its recorded time; an expectation may state an occurred time and a built-in member.
5. **Evaluators** (16 to 19): consultation is judged from committed state, per element; a function without `fresh` uses only an answer obtained for the request; a verdict too old is unknown; check 61 is stated once.
6. **Attribute corrections** (20 to 24): a correction relabels the current interval, opens none, and merges a relabelled interval with an equal one before it; every row whose value or `.held` it changes carries `.corrected_by`; its own row credits the corrected value; it is a write for `changed_since`; its event's provenance is `corrected`, an observation correction's too.
7. **Erasure and supersession** (25 to 39): it admits only what it leaves violated, on the object the invariant fails on; `not_erased` is decided with the generated guards, `clear` passing; a group's members are erased after the requested erasure, in id order, once each; a type declares at most one erasure, and one with nothing personal is passed over; erasing again runs the whole erasure again; the reach is per written value, and a type keeps an erasure once it needed one; a publish's redaction, a backfill and a caller's redacted event are stated; an assertion may not enter a superseding state.
8. **Proposals, flow changes, delivery and the API** (40 to 50): `Proposal` and `Subscription` are built-in types declared as `DeclarationChange` is, with named transitions a request takes; filing checks what a request checks short of state and guards; a refused approval is the proposed transition's attempt row; expiry is stated; a publish supersedes every other open change; the `DeclarationChange` lifecycle is printed as a flow declares one; a subscription starts at its creation, a cursor is exclusive, and lag has named thresholds a read returns; a refused `pull` is a value.
9. **The model's details** (51 to 58): uniqueness is partial where any attribute it ranges over is absent; the order of a type's invariants is stated whole; `changed_since` names contribute no object; `cascaded` lists the transitions the request took, an erasure's observation `forget`s included; an effect runs in step order; a bound name may not be a member or a reserved word; a loop over values runs in value order.
10. **Publishing and migration** (59 to 87): a rename writes no event; a backfill that changes nothing writes none; a migration writes a mirror; a standing admission is no decision; invariants are checked after all the mappings, over the objects they read; `CHECK`s are widened before and narrowed after; a span a publish opens is `.migrated`; the publish's own times, the impact categories, their keys and the refusals of an approval are stated; a stranded part, a mapping on a base, a set, a required attribute left absent and a retired type are settled; a span keeps the state it recorded.
11. **The import and the harness** (88 to 99): the built-in assertion evaluates no generated guard; a refresh compares per dimension and changes nothing only where nothing differs; the client skips an arrived batch; the changers and `recorded_from` of a port are stated; silent spans admit no overlap; the two new classes have a disposition; the import matches by its legacy key; the harness reads the installed version and exempts mirrors from its rule on parts.
12. **Metrics** (100 to 124): the closed-stretch rule is for a current span, by occurred time; `.held` on a port's span reads the index at its entry; a percentile is selected by row number; `int / int` truncates; a timestamp is held to the second; flags compare the rounded value on the `by` groups, over the rows a read selects; binding a broadcast input's missing dimension leaves it whole; `gaps` is a set of ids; a reader's window on an unwindowed metric and filter on a combined one are refused; rows sort in a stated order; a hop and an actor-naming dimension are defined; a would-be refusal is recorded only on a request that applies; an abstract base has no standard metrics.
13. **The scenarios stand where they agree with these decisions,** and the specification is corrected where a scenario computed what it meant (the closed stretch's current span).

## Alternatives rejected

- **For decision 1:** `time_in` following the closed stretch everywhere, which reads zero on a closed state and silently blocks a guard such as an auto-close a week after resolution; a meaning that depends on where the expression is written.
- **For decision 2:** the bound measured at approval, under which a proposal only ages past it and can never pass; a proposal with no occurred time, which overstates time in the earlier state by the approval's delay.
- **For decision 3:** the built-ins taking effect only at the next publish, which ties the engine's fixes to a deployment's next flow change and keeps old built-ins running indefinitely; an upgrade installing them unreported, as `storage-schema.md` §12 had it, which is the gap ADR-0105 closed.
- **For decisions 4 to 13:** the other reading each finding lists, in `cold-reading-2-review.md` §1 to §4.

## Consequences

The documents named above state each decision, area by area; the checkers and the reference runner learn what they check or run; and D505 to D513 record the areas, each resolved as its documents and tools change. ADR-0146's triage item 55 is corrected where it made `time_in` follow the closed stretch.
