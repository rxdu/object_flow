# Changelog

What each release of ObjectFlow's engine changed, for whoever runs it or builds on it. Releases follow semantic versioning (ADR-0180 decision 6), and from 1.0.0 the wire `/v1`, flow format 1 and the store's upgrade are held to ADR-0180's promise; a change that may refuse what an earlier release accepted is listed under its own heading. Each release is tagged `v<version>`, its service built from the tag with a SHA-256 checksum (`.github/workflows/release.yml`).

## Unreleased, toward 1.0.0

Release 1.0.0 is held until a production review, on the same 36.7k-object store as the first, finds no blocker or major (ADR-0183 decision 1; `docs/design/production-review.md`); the first three found some.

### Changes that may refuse what 0.2.0 accepted

- A proposal's `reject`, and its `withdraw` by any but its proposer, are held to the kinds of actor the proposed transition's `taken_by` names, refused as approving it would be, `invariant_violated`, `<transition>_taken_by`, `delegable`, in both backends, where an agent could reject what a person proposed (ADR-0191 decision 1).
- Money an effect writes past what an input holds, eighteen digits at its currency's minor unit, refuses its request `value_in_range`, in both backends, where an amount no request could send back was stored; a metric's `over` and a diagnostics' `window` are a positive duration, else `400`, where "-5 days" answered an empty page (ADR-0191 decision 3).
- An actor's identity names one actor: a creation or a write giving an identity another actor holds, of any type whose objects are actors, is refused `invariant_violated`, `identity_taken`, the holder named first, in both backends, where an agent issued a person's login was taken and the first made of the two answered for both; an identity holding a control, zero-width or bidirectional formatting character is `malformed`; and no module's invariant may be named `identity_taken` (ADR-0190 decision 4).
- A query filter nesting aggregates more than two deep, or writing a set of more than 1,000 members, is refused `malformed_filter`, where six nested aggregates ran past 30 s and a set of 10,000 values took 7.8 s, each holding a read's slot (ADR-0190 decision 3).
- A flow's source holding a bidirectional control or an invisible formatting character is refused, a `yaml` finding at its line, in both checkers, where a source could read otherwise than it shows (ADR-0190 decision 9).
- A body naming a key twice is `400`, where the last one won, a second `actor` changing who was recorded; `events` takes each position once, a repeat `400`, where one position named a thousand times answered 201 MB, in both backends (fifth production review, ADR-0190).
- Reads wait their turn in three pools: worklists (`query`, `available`, `pull`) and analytics (`metric`, `exceptions`, `diagnostics`, `export`), each at most `--max-reads` at once, and a change's dry run, a submit's, a preview's or a check's, one at a time; a `submit` or `refresh` of anything but a `DeclarationChange` takes no slot, where a claim's submit waited up to 29 s behind metrics and was refused `503`, and a worklist waited behind them too; four concurrent submits of a change peaked at 6.2 GB and six checks of one at 9.3 GB (fifth production review N-1, DRYRUN-MEMORY, ADR-0190).
- An effect writing a date or a time past the calendar, 0001-01-01 to 9999-12-31T23:59:59Z, refuses its request `value_in_range`, and a date moved past it has no value, in both backends, where `ends_at + 30 days` from the legacy "no end" 9999-12-31 stored year 10000, which no read took back, so its object, its type's scans and every later change of the flow answered `500` for good (fifth production review BRICK, ADR-0190).
- An import refuses a record's creation, entry into a state, interval or silent span after the import, as it refused an occurred time, and any of its times before 1900-01-01, a legacy system's empty date, where one in 0001 skewed a metric for good (fourth production review).
- A money input is a currency, one space and an amount, and a decimal one its digits, with nothing around them, as a date is read: `"USD  1.00"`, `"USD 1.00 "` and `" 1.00"` are `malformed`, in both backends, where they were taken (fourth production review).
- A query filter calling an evaluator, reading the request's `actor`, `inputs` or `this_event`, or dividing by a literal zero is refused `malformed_filter`, where it answered an empty page, and one with a literal past its type's range, where it answered `501` (fourth production review).
- `acknowledge` refuses `invalid_cursor` a cursor below 0 or past the log's last, where any integer was taken and one past the log skipped every later event; `export` refuses one past what the store has handed out, where a forged one answered an empty page for ever (fourth production review).
- An agent's generated tools offer a transition only where its `taken_by`, or its type's, names `agent`, and any other only for proposing where it is proposable: an agent held to its tools takes only what the flow names it for, where it was handed every transition a flow left open; the wire is as the flow says (ADR-0189). A flow whose agents take transitions names `agent` on them, as `reimbursement.yaml`'s `suggest_nature` now does.
- The reads that grow with the store, `query`, `available`, `metric`, `exceptions`, `diagnostics`, `pull`, `export` and a change's dry run, run at most `--max-reads` at once, 4 unless given, a read waiting its turn within its deadline and answered `503 read_timeout` past it, where any number ran at once and 32 metrics on six years took 3.3 GB (ADR-0189).
- The operator's retirement is refused `not_requestable`, as its creation is, where it was irreversible (ADR-0184).
- An import batch's `sequences` raise only a sequence a module declares, with a `last` that leaves it a next number, else the batch is refused, where `last` = i64::MAX was stored wrapped and stopped every write for good; a store an earlier release left so takes every write but a creation minting from that sequence, refused `value_in_range` (D607).
- A flow may declare who approves its versions, `publishing: { taken_by: [human] }`: read from the installed version, it refuses an approver of another kind `invariant_violated`, `publish_taken_by`, `delegable`, the store's operator always excepted; modules of a version that declare it differently are refused by both checkers; the samples declare `[human]` (ADR-0187 decision 1, third production review AGENT-APPROVE).
- A string value holds at most 64 KiB in UTF-8, past which an input or an import's value is refused `out_of_range`, alike in the reference runner; `/health` answers `503 disk_low` below 64 MiB free on the store's volume (ADR-0188).
- A `decimal(p,s)` holds `p` digits, as the syntax says: an input past them is refused `out_of_range` and an effect's write `value_in_range`, alike in the reference runner, where 18 were taken whatever `p`; an `idempotency_key` holds at most 256 bytes and a request's `context` 16 KiB, past which they are `400` (third production review, minors).
- A store whose file fails SQLite's quick check does not start, where a copy restored beside another state's `-wal` answered health while its reads failed; a prune where the service has no retention for it is refused `no_retention`, where it said `inside_retention`; an upgrade asked of a store with none due is refused to anyone but an operator (`409`) (third production review).
- A date or a timestamp is read as RFC 3339 writes it, each field its digits exactly and the year 0001 to 9999, at UTC: a space, a sign, a one-digit month, a year past 9999, the year 0 and the offset `+0000` are refused `malformed`, alike in the reference runner, which took a one-digit month and any offset.
- A request's own `principal` is read, typed and checked as the actor is, a principal naming no one refused `actor_known`, where it was taken and dropped; it and `actor.principal` must agree (third production review PRINCIPAL).
- A change's dry run that passes `--read-timeout`, a `submit`'s or a `publish` preview's, is answered `503 read_timeout` and records nothing, where a submit's was computed again under the writer's lock and a preview held the writer throughout (third production review PUBLISH-SCALE, D604).
- An effect whose arithmetic of present values has no value, an integer past 64 bits, a negation of the least or a division by zero among them, refuses its request as the generated clause `value_in_range`, `unsatisfied` with the remedy `unreachable_from_here`, naming the object, where the core answered `501 not run` and the reference runner stored the value; a condition may not be named `value_in_range` (ADR-0185, production review F5). The least integer leaves the wire as a string, as every integer past 2⁵³ does, where a release build sent it as a number (D601).
- `events` takes 1,000 positions at most.
- A file that holds something other than a store, another application's database among them, is refused with `--create` too, where it was given the store's tables and served.
- A description nesting its collections deeper than 64 levels is refused, a `yaml` finding, where 300,000 levels aborted the service (D598).

- A deployment's store path that holds no store, absent or empty, is refused unless the start gives `--create`, so a mistyped path is never served as an empty store (ADR-0183 decision 3).
- `dry_run` and `propose` take `true` or `false`, and `inputs` an object; anything else is `400`, where `"dry_run": "true"` was read as false and installed a version (ADR-0183 decision 6). Every other key is typed too: a value of another JSON type is `400`, where a numeric `filter` returned every object unfiltered, and `actor` takes `id` and `principal` alone.
- `maintain` names an actor the store knows, as a request does: one it does not know is refused `actor_known`, and one in a final state `actor_active`, where any name was taken (ADR-0183 decision 5).
- An expression nests at most 64 levels and its tree is at most 128 deep, in a request's filter and in a module, where a deep filter overflowed the stack and stopped the service (production review F1).
- A decimal written by an effect, an input or a default is rounded half to even to its declared scale, as `declaration-syntax.md` §8.3 states, where the core stored unscaled units (production review F4).
- A request whose actor or principal is in a final state is refused `actor_active`; a type may declare `taken_by` for every transition declaring none; a query's filter may read any member of its type; every built-in attribute holding JSON is answered as JSON (ADR-0182).
- `taken_by` marks any transition a request takes; a refusal names no object that does not exist; a query's filter is typed against its literals; an attribute named `<reference>_id` beside a single reference is refused (ADR-0181).
- `query`, `available`, `pull` and `export` answer pages of 100 unless asked otherwise, a client that ignores the cursor reading the first page (ADR-0179 decision 5).

### Added

- The checkers' notice `rule_changed`, naming against the previous version each type's conditions and invariants removed or rewritten and guards that no longer refuse, so whoever approves a version reads them (ADR-0191 decision 2).
- `/metrics`' `objectflow_build_info` and `objectflow_start_time_seconds`; `WATCHDOG=1` under a unit's `WatchdogSec`; a `malformed_filter` page's `reason`; at the start, a `--create` over a store that exists, an unclean last stop and an evaluator's token over plain http beyond loopback said, and argument errors stamped (ADR-0191 decision 4).
- The checker's notices `unbounded_reach`, for an erasure's reach a limit may stop, a set no invariant holds within the limit, and `publishing_open`, for a version declaring an agent kind of actor and no rule for who approves its versions; the samples bound their erasures' sets and `people.yaml` declares `publishing: { taken_by: [human] }` (ADR-0190 decisions 7, 10).
- `--metrics-bind <host:port>`, serving `GET /metrics` and `GET /health` alone; `sd_notify` `READY=1` and `STOPPING=1` for a unit of `Type=notify`; a fault's `code` beside its reason, which a client branches on; `/health`'s `503` for a store file moved or replaced (`store_replaced`, every write refused alike), a blocked log (`log_blocked`) and writes stalled behind another process's lock (`writes_stalled`); `/metrics`' verdicts by kind, evaluator failures, dropped log lines and a duration histogram by route (fourth production review).
- The checker's notices `agent_open`, for each module's transitions an agent may take on the wire where the version declares an agent kind of actor, and `taken_by_dropped`, for a `taken_by` or `publishing` rule a version drops or widens against the previous one, in both checkers (ADR-0189).
- `--max-reads <n>` and `/metrics`' `objectflow_reads_running`, `objectflow_reads_waiting` and `objectflow_reads_most` (ADR-0189); `--help` names `--evaluator-token` and `--max-reads`, where it lacked the first (fourth production review).
- `--evaluator-token <name>=<file>`: an evaluator's bearer token, read at start from a file its owner alone reads and sent with every call, never logged; `/health`'s `disk_free_bytes` and `/metrics`' `objectflow_disk_free_bytes`, the free space on the store's volume (ADR-0188).
- `get` and `history` follow a supersession with `"follow": true`, which the wire listed and neither backend built, the key taken and ignored: `get` answers where the chain ends, `history` the whole group's events in position order; `follow` of another type is a `400`.
- `/metrics` gives the store's file and its `-wal` in bytes; the `-wal` is cut back to 64 MiB when a checkpoint empties it, where it grew to six times the store and stayed; a start that cannot make its store or socket, or bind its address, names the path (third production review N6).
- `--socket <path>`: the service on a Unix socket, made `0660`, in an address's place, so the socket's owner and group are its only callers (ADR-0183 decision 4).
- One line on stderr for each answer, its time, method, route, status and duration; `GET /metrics`, the answers by route and status, the store's version and the clock's lead, in Prometheus's text format; `/health` reading the store's file and answering `clock_lead_seconds` (ADR-0183 decision 8).
- On SIGTERM or SIGINT the service takes no new connection, answers the requests in flight for up to 30 s and exits, where it stopped at once (ADR-0183 decision 9).
- `/health` and `/metrics` say how long the writer has been held and the requests in flight, `/metrics` the process's resident memory; `/health` is `503` while an upgrade is due; a 5xx's reason is logged (OBSERVE-2).
- A clean stop carries the write-ahead log into the store's file; a socket a live service answers on is refused, never taken over; start errors are sentences.
- At `--max-connections` the oldest connection answering nothing is closed to make room, where silent connections reset every newcomer, `/health` among them; a newcomer is answered `503 busy` only when every connection is answering a request; `/health` says `connections_open` and `/metrics` the connections open, closed to make room and refused (ADR-0186, third production review C-1).
- `--idle-timeout`, `--max-connections` and `--read-timeout`: a connection whose request does not arrive is closed, a body that does not is `408`, connections are capped, and a deployment's read passing its deadline is stopped and answered `503 read_timeout` (ADR-0184).
- A request that panics holding the writer no longer leaves every later write `500`: the next write takes the lock back and reopens the store from its file.
- `--version`.
- The checkers notice a correction's free-text reason on a type an erasure reaches, `reason_kept`, since its event keeps the reason and an erasure leaves it (production review F7).
- A refusal tells its caller what its remedy points at (ADR-0179).
- The wire's baseline, `docs/guides/wire-v1.json`, and format 1's frozen corpus, each held by a check (ADR-0180 decision 7).

### Fixed

- A derived time past the calendar is answered `null` in both backends, where the core answered year 2,739,933 and the reference runner answered `500`; `/health` answers a store gone from its path `503 store_replaced`, as a write is, where it answered `500` (ADR-0191 decisions 3, 4).
- A proposal names its proposer by identity in both backends, as every event names its actor, where the core kept what the request named and the reference runner the actor's object id (D611).
- The core's checker reads a byte-order mark at a source's start as no part of it, as the reference checker does, where it took the mark into the first key and refused the file (D610).
- A correction may take `taken_by`, its own or its type's, where the checker refused it as writing more than it corrects, so a type's `taken_by: [human]` could not hold on a type with a correction and an agent rewrote a recorded bill, in both checkers (fifth production review NEW-CORRECTION, ADR-0190). A log whose reader has gone drops its lines, where `eprintln!` panicked on the broken pipe, losing answers to writes already committed and holding `/health` at `log_blocked` until a restart.
- A start refused for a store failing SQLite's quick check leaves the store's file and its `-wal` as they were: every connection the start opens before the store is served, the one reading the store's release and the check's, carries no log into the file as it closes, where a refused start wrote another state's `-wal` into a restored copy and emptied it (fourth production review). **Corrected:** the first fix, at `879fb2c`, covered the check's connection alone, and this entry, `integrate.md` §9 and the start's message claimed the copy untouched while the release's connection still wrote it, as the fifth production review measured (D609).
- A full disk answers a write `503 disk_full`, which a client may retry, where it answered `500`, and a fault of one code is written to the log at most once a second, the rest counted; every log line is stamped, the start's among them; a log no one reads holds one line and then drops lines, counted, so no request waits on it, where the service stopped at its next line; listening beyond loopback is said at the start; an evaluator's address is logged with its password hidden; what a run of short requests freed is given back once the service is idle (fourth production review).
- A publish report shows each of the checks' findings, where it kept the first of each code, so a second module's notice or a second refusal of one code was never shown (D608).

### Performance

- A supersession group's erasure finds each member's predecessors by an index on their successor's column, which a store opened without it is given, where it read every superseding type whole for each member, 1.43 s for a group of 41 (ADR-0191 decision 5).
- An erasure reads the events it walks by the log's indexes, its object's own, those they caused and those that called them, where it read the whole log under the writer (fourth production review).
- `pull` and `export` read a page from their cursor by position, never the whole event log and every object: on six years of claims an empty pull takes 1 ms and 15 MB where 1.1 s and 462 MB, and twenty export pages of 1,000 1.4 s where 90.6 s and 1.9 GB; a subscription's lag reads its first pending event alone (fourth production review FEED).
- An external identifier is indexed whatever its marking, and a store opened without the index is given it: a go-live's import batch takes the same time at its last batch as its first, where 3,000 objects took 1.6 s at the start and 4.2 s at 36k objects (D606).
- After a publish, an import batch or any request of half a second or more, the service gives back to the system what it freed, which glibc kept: ten seconds after a publish it holds 25 MB on 37k objects where it held 975 MB, and 36 MB on 110k where 2.95 GB; `objectflow_memory_given_back_total` counts it (ADR-0187 decision 2).
- A query's page in creation order reads from its cursor, a chunk at a time, where each page read and filtered its type whole: a filter on a derived attribute read page by page over 12k claims took 1.6 s where 44.4 s, and its first page 0.03 s where 1.03 s (third production review READ-PAGES).
- A publish's dry run is linear in the store, where checking each object's optional identifiers swept every object without one: on 110k objects a publish adding one attribute submitted in 6.4 s where 41.5 s, and its approval held writes 9.1 s where 44.5 s (D605).
- A submit's dry run runs beside the writes, where every write waited for it: 83 s for a module of 3,000 types, which also checks ten times faster (D599, D600).
- A person's or an agent's submit of a version, which resolved its submitter by reading every actor for each object, took 6 s where it passed 20 minutes on 37k objects (D594); a per-person metric and a filter on a derived attribute 1 to 1.5 s where 77 to 83 s (D595); `available` reads only the objects its transition is taken from (D596); an erasure's truncation of the write-ahead log waits on no reader, where it made every write take 5 s while a read was open (D597).
- An attribute marked `indexed` has an index, which no release before made, and opening a store makes one it lacks: the go-live import of 36k objects took 36 s where 145 s (D593).
- Reads by index, not by reading a type whole: on a store of 36.7k objects a `get` took 1 ms where it took 184 ms, a 50-claim worklist 11 ms where 737 ms, a write about 10 ms where about 200 ms, and a publish adding one attribute 4 s of dry run and 5 s of approval where 214 s and 220 s held every write (production review PERF-SCAN, PUBLISH-FREEZE).

### Security

- The write-ahead log is cut back within 5 s of an erasure, and whenever it passes 64 MiB, new long reads held back while those running end: reads that always overlapped kept an erasure's values in it, and kept it growing, 3.2 GB in 150 s; on six years at 124 writes a second beside two six-second reads it now peaks at about 300 MiB (fourth production review).
- An idempotency key whose request an erasure redacted answers its first verdict whatever is sent under it, where it answered `409` to a wrong guess of the erased value and replayed the right one (fourth production review). A fault's reason is written to the log at most 1 KiB long, where a filter of 1.9 MB was written whole.
- A release's workflow refuses a tag whose commit's `check-all` has not passed, and runs the core's and the service's tests on the runner that builds the binary it ships (third production review).
- A store `--create` makes is `0600`, its `-wal` and `-shm` too; the service says at its start where a store's file is readable by others.
- An evaluator's failure is logged by its function, never its arguments or the endpoint's answer.
- An erasure leaves nothing it erased in the store's files (ADR-0181 decision 4).

## 0.2.0, 2026-10-10

- The store records the engine release that made it and each that upgraded it, and every operation reads it, so no copy of an earlier release serves a store once it is upgraded; `POST /v1/upgrade` carries a store from 0.1.0 (ADR-0177).

## 0.1.0

- The engine's Rust core and its HTTP service, first numbered 2026-10-04 (ADR-0153): a sandbox's store, and from 2026-10-10 a deployment's on one SQLite file (ADR-0174), with evaluators over HTTP (ADR-0175) and reads beside a writer (ADR-0178). Every store made before 0.2.0 is of this release.
